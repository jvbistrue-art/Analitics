Now it's time for us to learn a new interval. Remember, we talked about our intervals before — we talked about the second, the third, the fourth, and the fifth. An interval, I will remind you, is the distance between two sounds. And the distance can be a short distance or a long distance, and all these intervals have different names. In English, we name the intervals according to the English alphabet. So for example, if you have a second, then you will have two letter names involved in the interval, in alphabetical order. If you have three letter names involved in the interval — for example, A, B, and C — then the distance between A and C is going to be called a third. If you have four letter names involved in the interval in alphabetical order, like A, B, C, D, the distance between A and D is going to be called the fourth. The same you do with the fifth: A, B, C, D, E — the distance from A to E is called the fifth, because you have five letters involved in that interval. Now a sixth will follow the same logic: A, B, C, D, E, F. So you have the distance between A to F, which is called a sixth. So you will have A–F, A–F. So this interval is called a sixth. Let's look at another one: B–G. This interval is also called a sixth.
